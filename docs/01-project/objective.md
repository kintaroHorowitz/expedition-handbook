# Objective

The primary objective is to complete the approved Tour des Écrins itinerary in
the final week of June 2027 over six days, while preserving the health,
durability, judgement and enjoyment needed for a successful expedition.

!!! info "Canonical planning metrics"
    The timing, duration and body-weight values repeated on this page are for
    readability. Their canonical structured source is `data/profile.yml`.
    Mutable current-weight data is intentionally not repeated here.

## Decision framework

- A **goal** describes the overall outcome: a safe, durable and worthwhile
  expedition.
- A **target** is a measurable planning aim, such as six days or an 8 kg pack.
  It supports the goal and may be changed when evidence warrants it.
- An **assumption** is a planning condition that is not guaranteed, including
  expected route access, weather and normal logistics.
- A **hard safety constraint** overrides completion, duration, weight and
  performance targets.

## Planning targets

- **Timing:** the final week of June 2027.
- **Duration:** six days.
- **Body weight:** a planning range of 105–110 kg.
- **Backpack:** no more than 8 kg starting total weight for a normal stage.

The backpack target includes the pack, carried equipment, lunch food, snacks,
stove and fuel if used, and carried water. Worn clothing, footwear and items
carried on the body are excluded.

The historical 15 kg Tour du Mont Blanc starting pack is a reference value only.
It is not the Tour des Écrins target.

## Success criteria

Success is assessed across completion, health and durability, performance, and
expedition quality. No single metric determines the result.

### Completion

- **Primary completion:** complete the full approved Tour des Écrins itinerary
  and intended loop.
- **Qualified completion:** complete a pre-approved or safety-driven variant
  that preserves the essential route.
- **Partial completion:** record a substantial truncation that does not complete
  the intended loop accurately as partial.

A partial completion may still represent a successful expedition from a health,
safety and decision-quality perspective. A safety-driven route or duration
change is not, by itself, a decision failure.

### Health and durability

- Remain able to start successive stages responsibly.
- Avoid a progressive loss of basic function or decision quality.
- Recover sufficiently between stages to make a responsible next-day decision.
- Do not continue merely to protect the six-day schedule when symptoms,
  recovery or fatigue materially reduce the safety margin.

These are planning and safety criteria, not medical diagnosis or treatment.
Detailed green, amber and red adaptation rules belong with the project risks and
adaptation rules defined under CDB-15.

### Performance

- Maintain a sustainable effort across six consecutive days.
- Preserve useful reserve for changing mountain conditions and logistics.
- Apply the tested fuelling and hydration approach consistently.
- Maintain recovery and sound decision quality rather than maximizing any
  single stage.

Exact distance, elevation gain, pace and stage-time thresholds are intentionally
not defined here.

### Enjoyment and expedition quality

- Preserve enough physical and mental reserve to appreciate the route.
- Avoid making the schedule so aggressive that recovery dominates the trip.
- Complete a short post-expedition review of enjoyment, stress, route quality
  and whether six days was appropriate.

Enjoyment remains qualitative and is not reduced to a numeric score.

## Role of body weight

The 105–110 kg range is a load-management and durability target, not the main
success criterion. Reaching it does not prove readiness, and being outside it
does not by itself determine whether the expedition can proceed. Demonstrated
multi-day capacity, recovery, equipment, conditions and judgement remain more
direct readiness evidence.

## Readiness before departure

Before departure:

- Complete at least three consecutive representative hiking days while carrying
  the approved expedition pack.
- Show no progressive loss of basic function or decision quality during that
  block, with enough recovery to start each next day responsibly.
- Test equipment, footwear, pack, fuelling and hydration in representative
  conditions.
- Confirm that no unresolved issue materially compromises consecutive mountain
  days.
- Demonstrate sustainable multi-day effort rather than relying on one-day
  performance.
- Re-evaluate the six-day plan after the representative block.

The user, as product owner, makes the formal decision to retain or change the
six-day objective about two weeks before departure. A final safety and logistics
re-check occurs within 48 hours of departure.

## When to change the six-day objective

Change or reconsider the duration when:

- the representative multi-day block does not support responsible continuation;
- symptoms, recovery, function or fatigue materially reduce the safety margin;
- weather, route conditions, access or logistics make six stages unreasonable;
- the approved expedition pack cannot be carried responsibly in representative
  conditions; or
- a longer plan would materially improve durability or expedition quality.

!!! warning "Safety overrides the plan"
    Safety constraints override the full-route objective, the six-day target,
    body-weight and backpack targets, performance goals, bookings and other sunk
    costs. Stopping, delaying, shortening or rerouting must remain available
    throughout the expedition.
