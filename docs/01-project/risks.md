# Adaptation framework

This framework defines how preparation continues, changes, pauses, or leads to
reconsideration of the expedition objective when reality differs from the plan.
It is an operational decision framework, not a medical guide, rehabilitation
protocol, detailed training plan, or diagnostic framework.

!!! warning "Limits of this framework"
    This page does not diagnose conditions, prescribe treatment or reinterpret
    professional medical advice. Health concerns that require professional
    assessment are handled outside the handbook.

## Guiding principles

- Safety and responsible continuation take priority over the calendar, targets,
  bookings and sunk costs.
- Decisions use current evidence rather than assuming that the original plan
  remains appropriate.
- When evidence is incomplete, choose the safer reversible response rather than
  assuming continuation is appropriate.
- Adapt the smallest relevant part of the plan first, unless the evidence
  requires wider change.
- Do not compensate automatically for reduced or missed preparation.
- Record what evidence is needed before increasing demand or resuming a paused
  activity.
- A classification may change when new evidence becomes available.

## Roles and decision inputs

The product owner remains responsible for expedition planning decisions. A
review may be triggered by personal observations, training or journal records,
changes in work or family circumstances, weather and route information,
equipment checks, logistics, or advice from an appropriate professional.

Professional advice is important new evidence. Restrictions or advice from a
health professional carry appropriately strong weight in health-related
decisions. The handbook must not reinterpret that advice, turn it into a
diagnosis, or substitute its own treatment decision.

## Adaptation philosophy

An adaptation may continue the plan with monitoring, reduce demand, replace an
activity, postpone it, pause it, return preparation to an earlier phase, seek
appropriate review, or reconsider the route, duration, timing or departure.
The response should match the affected risk and preserve options where
possible.

The expedition objective normally remains unchanged while a bounded issue can
be managed without undermining readiness or safety. It is reconsidered when a
risk persists, compounds with other risks, removes necessary readiness
evidence, or materially compromises expedition safety or feasibility.

## Green, amber and red decision model

Green, amber and red are operational planning classifications. They are not
medical classifications and must not be used to diagnose a condition.

### Green — continue and monitor

Use Green when current evidence supports the planned activity and phase.
Continue with normal observation and review. The expedition objective remains
unchanged, but Green does not by itself justify increasing demand.

### Amber — adapt and reassess

Use Amber when evidence is uncertain, a negative pattern may be developing, or
the available margin has materially decreased. Do not progress automatically.
Reduce, replace, postpone or simplify the affected activity and reassess the
relevant evidence.

Every Amber decision must answer: **What evidence is required before returning
to Green?** Until that evidence exists, retain the safer reversible response.

### Red — stop and escalate

Use Red when continuation is not responsibly supported or when safety or
feasibility is materially compromised. Stop the affected activity or plan and
escalate to the appropriate decision maker or professional source.

Every Red decision must answer: **What evidence is required before the affected
activity may resume?** A scheduled date alone is never sufficient evidence.

## Risk categories

The categories below apply the same decision structure to health and recovery,
preparation continuity, external conditions, equipment and logistics, and
expedition feasibility.

## Injury and rehabilitation

- **Review triggers:** Recurrence or worsening of the Achilles limitation, a
  new injury concern, loss of normal function, an adverse pattern after
  activity, or new professional advice.
- **Green interpretation:** Current activity is tolerated, recovery supports
  continuation, and no new restriction changes the plan.
- **Amber interpretation:** Tolerance or function is uncertain, a negative
  pattern may be developing, or the available margin has decreased.
- **Red interpretation:** Continuing the affected activity is not responsibly
  supported, basic function is materially compromised, or professional advice
  restricts continuation.
- **Default decision:** Reduce, replace or pause the affected activity while
  evidence is incomplete; do not test uncertainty by increasing demand.
- **Permitted responses:** Continue with monitoring, reduce or replace the
  activity, pause progression, return to an earlier phase, or seek appropriate
  professional review.
- **Evidence required for progression/resumption:** Current tolerance,
  functional evidence, recovery after representative activity, and compliance
  with any applicable professional restrictions or advice.
- **Objective implication:** Keep the objective unchanged while the issue is
  bounded; reconsider timing, duration or departure if it persistently removes
  necessary readiness evidence or safety margin.
- **Detailed owner:** A future rehabilitation document and appropriate
  professional guidance own specific rehabilitation and treatment decisions.

## Illness

- **Review triggers:** Illness that affects normal activity, recovery, basic
  function or the ability to judge readiness, or advice to restrict activity.
- **Green interpretation:** Normal activity and recovery are supported by
  current evidence, with no relevant restriction.
- **Amber interpretation:** The effect of illness is uncertain, recovery is
  incomplete, or normal preparation would add avoidable strain.
- **Red interpretation:** Training or expedition activity is not responsibly
  supported, or health-professional advice restricts it.
- **Default decision:** Pause or reduce affected activity when the effect is
  unclear rather than using training to test whether illness has passed.
- **Permitted responses:** Rest from affected activity, replace it only when
  appropriate, delay progression, and seek medical review when concerns persist,
  worsen, or cannot be interpreted safely.
- **Evidence required for progression/resumption:** Evidence that normal
  function and recovery have returned sufficiently for the proposed activity,
  together with observance of professional advice.
- **Objective implication:** Short interruptions need not change the objective;
  prolonged or late illness may require changes to readiness, timing or
  departure.
- **Detailed owner:** Future health and safety guidance owns specific procedures;
  diagnosis and treatment remain outside the handbook.

## Fatigue, recovery and overload

- **Review triggers:** A worsening recovery pattern, repeated inability to
  tolerate planned work, loss of normal function or decision quality, or
  accumulating demands across training and daily life.
- **Green interpretation:** Recovery supports the current phase and successive
  activities remain responsible.
- **Amber interpretation:** Recovery is uncertain, performance or function is
  deteriorating, or combined demands have materially reduced the margin.
- **Red interpretation:** Continued loading is not responsibly supported or
  basic function and decision quality are materially compromised.
- **Default decision:** Reduce demand and hold progression until the recovery
  pattern is understood.
- **Permitted responses:** Reduce, replace or postpone training, add recovery,
  return to an earlier phase, or seek appropriate review when the pattern
  persists or has an unclear health component.
- **Evidence required for progression/resumption:** A stable recovery pattern,
  restored ability to tolerate representative activity, and sufficient reserve
  for the next planned demand.
- **Objective implication:** Preserve the objective when recovery responds to a
  bounded adaptation; reconsider it if overload prevents representative
  readiness work or persists near departure.
- **Detailed owner:** Future training and recovery documents own detailed load
  changes.

## Weight changes

- **Review triggers:** An unexpectedly rapid, unexplained or sustained weight
  change; a trend that appears to impair recovery, function or preparation; or
  conflict between weight management and readiness.
- **Green interpretation:** The observed trend remains compatible with health,
  recovery, function and sustainable preparation.
- **Amber interpretation:** The trend or its effect is uncertain, or recovery,
  function, enjoyment or training consistency may be deteriorating.
- **Red interpretation:** Continuing the current approach is not responsibly
  supported, or professional advice requires it to stop or change.
- **Default decision:** Stop increasing the weight-management demand and review
  the available evidence; do not infer safety from an unapproved rate.
- **Permitted responses:** Maintain, reduce or pause the weight-management
  demand, prioritize recovery and preparation continuity, or seek appropriate
  professional review.
- **Evidence required for progression/resumption:** A trend and supporting
  recovery, function and preparation evidence that justify the next decision,
  plus compliance with applicable professional advice.
- **Objective implication:** Body weight alone does not determine success or
  readiness. Reconsider the plan only when the broader effects materially
  undermine readiness or safety.
- **Detailed owner:** `data/profile.yml` owns structured body-weight values. A
  future privacy-safe measurement history and nutrition document would own
  detailed observations and strategy.

No kilogram-per-week, percentage, calorie-deficit or target-rate threshold is
defined by this framework.

## Training interruptions

- **Review triggers:** Missed preparation, loss of continuity, travel, illness,
  injury, facility constraints, or any gap that invalidates an assumption about
  current readiness.
- **Green interpretation:** The interruption does not materially affect current
  evidence or the next planned demand.
- **Amber interpretation:** Continuity or readiness is uncertain and normal
  progression may no longer be justified.
- **Red interpretation:** Resuming the planned level is not responsibly
  supported or essential readiness work can no longer be completed safely.
- **Default decision:** Resume from demonstrated current capacity, not from the
  calendar position, and do not compress missed work.
- **Permitted responses:** Repeat, extend or return to an earlier phase; reduce
  or replace the next demand; or revise the remaining preparation sequence.
- **Evidence required for progression/resumption:** Current tolerance,
  consistency and recovery at the level from which preparation will resume.
- **Objective implication:** Preserve the objective after a recoverable gap;
  reconsider it when the interruption removes essential readiness evidence or
  leaves insufficient safe preparation time.
- **Detailed owner:** The relevant future phase training page owns the resumed
  training detail.

## Work and family constraints

- **Review triggers:** Reduced available time, disrupted recovery opportunity,
  competing responsibilities, travel, or preparation becoming incompatible
  with important commitments.
- **Green interpretation:** The plan remains sustainable alongside current
  responsibilities.
- **Amber interpretation:** Continuity, recovery or personal margin is being
  eroded, even if individual sessions remain possible.
- **Red interpretation:** The plan creates an unacceptable conflict or cannot be
  carried out responsibly under current constraints.
- **Default decision:** Simplify and protect essential continuity rather than
  adding pressure or compensating later.
- **Permitted responses:** Reduce frequency or complexity, replace activities,
  postpone non-essential work, extend a phase, or redesign the preparation
  sequence.
- **Evidence required for progression/resumption:** A sustainable schedule with
  adequate recovery opportunity and agreement that the next demand is
  realistically supportable.
- **Objective implication:** Keep the objective while constraints are temporary
  and manageable; reconsider timing or scope when they persist or prevent
  readiness.
- **Detailed owner:** Future detailed training plans own scheduling options;
  private family information does not belong in the handbook.

## Weather and seasonal conditions

- **Review triggers:** Severe or unsuitable weather, heat, snow, unsafe terrain,
  access restrictions, or conditions that make the planned activity
  unrepresentative or unsafe.
- **Green interpretation:** Current forecasts, terrain and access support the
  planned activity with the intended safety margin.
- **Amber interpretation:** Conditions or forecasts are uncertain, deteriorating
  or reduce the available margin.
- **Red interpretation:** Conditions, snow or access make the planned activity
  or expedition plan unsafe or infeasible.
- **Default decision:** Do not depend on favourable conditions that have not
  been confirmed; choose a safer location, substitute or postponement.
- **Permitted responses:** Change timing or location, replace or shorten the
  activity, postpone it, or revise the route or expedition schedule.
- **Evidence required for progression/resumption:** Current authoritative
  conditions, forecasts, access information and route-specific evidence that
  support the proposed activity.
- **Objective implication:** Local training substitutions normally leave the
  objective unchanged; unresolved expedition conditions may require rerouting,
  delaying, shortening or stopping.
- **Detailed owner:** A future mountain-conditions and route-safety document
  owns detailed weather and snow procedures.

## Equipment failures

- **Review triggers:** Missing, damaged, unreliable, unsuitable or untested
  equipment, including a failure during representative use.
- **Green interpretation:** Required equipment is available, serviceable and
  demonstrated in relevant use.
- **Amber interpretation:** Reliability, fit, suitability or replacement timing
  is uncertain.
- **Red interpretation:** Essential equipment is unavailable or failed and the
  affected activity cannot be performed responsibly without it.
- **Default decision:** Do not depend on uncertain equipment; replace, repair,
  retest or change the affected activity.
- **Permitted responses:** Repair or replace equipment, use a tested alternative,
  postpone equipment-dependent work, or revise the activity or expedition plan.
- **Evidence required for progression/resumption:** A successful inspection and
  representative test of the equipment or approved alternative.
- **Objective implication:** Isolated failures leave the objective unchanged
  when resolved; unresolved essential equipment may change the route, schedule
  or departure decision.
- **Detailed owner:** A future equipment document owns inventories, tests,
  maintenance and replacement procedures.

## Logistics and accommodation

- **Review triggers:** Refuge availability or booking changes, transport or
  access disruption, resupply uncertainty, or loss of another dependency on
  which the itinerary relies.
- **Green interpretation:** Required logistics and accommodation are confirmed
  and compatible with the plan.
- **Amber interpretation:** A dependency is uncertain or an alternative has not
  been confirmed.
- **Red interpretation:** An essential dependency is unavailable and no
  responsible alternative supports the planned itinerary.
- **Default decision:** Do not depend on an unconfirmed refuge, transport link,
  resupply point or access assumption.
- **Permitted responses:** Confirm an alternative, revise a stage or route,
  change timing or duration, postpone departure, or remove the unsupported
  dependency.
- **Evidence required for progression/resumption:** Confirmed availability,
  access and a workable alternative where the original dependency changed.
- **Objective implication:** Keep the objective when a safe alternative
  preserves it; reconsider route, duration or timing when essential logistics
  cannot be restored.
- **Detailed owner:** A future logistics document owns bookings, transport,
  refuge and resupply procedures.

## Expedition schedule changes

- **Review triggers:** Accumulated readiness gaps, route or refuge changes,
  weather and snow, access constraints, or another risk that threatens the
  planned timing or duration.
- **Green interpretation:** Current evidence continues to support the approved
  objective and departure window.
- **Amber interpretation:** The schedule remains possible but its readiness,
  safety or feasibility margin is materially reduced or uncertain.
- **Red interpretation:** The planned timing, duration or itinerary is not
  responsibly supported.
- **Default decision:** Preserve optionality and prepare alternatives rather
  than forcing the original schedule.
- **Permitted responses:** Retain the schedule with monitoring, change duration
  or route, postpone departure, or stop the expedition plan.
- **Evidence required for progression/resumption:** Current readiness,
  conditions, access and logistics evidence supporting the selected schedule
  and itinerary.
- **Objective implication:** This category is the formal point at which timing,
  duration, route or departure may be reconsidered under the principles owned
  by the objective page.
- **Detailed owner:** The [objective](objective.md) owns expedition goals and
  readiness principles; future route and logistics documents own alternatives.

## Psychological and motivation

- **Review triggers:** Sustained loss of motivation, burnout, repeated
  avoidance, preparation becoming an excessive burden, or a major loss of
  enjoyment.
- **Green interpretation:** Preparation remains purposeful, sustainable and
  compatible with preserving expedition quality.
- **Amber interpretation:** Motivation or enjoyment is deteriorating, avoidance
  is recurring, or the plan is becoming disproportionately burdensome.
- **Red interpretation:** Continuing the current plan is not responsibly
  sustainable or is causing a major conflict with wellbeing or important life
  priorities.
- **Default decision:** Reassess goals, load, continuity, recovery and plan
  design rather than responding with more training pressure.
- **Permitted responses:** Simplify or reduce preparation, change activity mix,
  add recovery, revisit goals, pause the affected plan, or seek appropriate
  support.
- **Evidence required for progression/resumption:** Evidence that the revised
  approach is sustainable, purposeful and restores sufficient willingness and
  margin for continued preparation.
- **Objective implication:** Preserve the objective when a bounded redesign
  restores sustainability; reconsider it when the burden or loss of enjoyment
  remains material.
- **Detailed owner:** Future training and journal documents may own plan-design
  options and privacy-safe observations; professional support remains outside
  the handbook.

## Escalation rules

- Green conditions remain under routine monitoring.
- Every Amber decision identifies the evidence required to return to Green and
  sets a review point before progression.
- Every Red decision stops the affected activity or plan, identifies the
  evidence required for resumption, and escalates to the appropriate source.
- Persistent, worsening or unclear health concerns require appropriate medical
  review rather than handbook interpretation.
- Professional restrictions remain controlling evidence until appropriately
  revised by the relevant professional source.
- Multiple Amber conditions may justify a Red decision when their combined
  effect materially removes the safety or feasibility margin.
- Emergency procedures and contacts are outside this framework and must not be
  invented here.

## Review cadence

Review the framework:

- at the monthly checkpoints defined by the [preparation timeline](timeline.md);
- whenever a material change or warning signal occurs;
- before progressing into a more demanding preparation phase;
- after a material interruption and before normal progression resumes;
- during the formal readiness decision approximately two weeks before
  departure; and
- during the final safety and logistics review within 48 hours of departure.

These are decision points, not a training schedule. An event-triggered review
does not wait for the next scheduled checkpoint.

## Ownership boundaries

The [preparation timeline](timeline.md) owns phase sequencing and indicative
periods. The [objective](objective.md) owns expedition objectives, success
criteria and readiness principles. This page owns adaptation and escalation
decisions.

Future detailed training, rehabilitation, equipment, logistics,
mountain-condition, safety and journal documents will own their specific
procedures. They are not created by CDB-15. A structured risk register may be
introduced later, after enough real adaptation decisions exist to justify its
fields and workflow; this page remains the current narrative framework.
